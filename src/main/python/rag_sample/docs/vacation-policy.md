# Vacation and Paid Time Off Policy

Owner: People Operations
Document ID: HR-POL-011
Last reviewed: 2026-01-15

## Entitlement

Every full-time employee of Loan Rangers accrues 28 calendar days of paid
vacation per year, accruing at 2.33 days per completed calendar month.
Part-time employees accrue pro rata based on contracted hours.

Unused days may be carried over into the next year, but the carry-over
balance expires on 31 March. Any balance still unused on 1 April is
forfeited without compensation.

## How to request vacation

1. Open the People Portal and submit form **HRP-204** ("Absence Request").
2. Select the absence type `VACATION_PAID`.
3. Your line manager receives the request and must approve or decline it
   within five working days.
4. Once approved, the absence is pushed to the shared team calendar
   automatically. There is no need to create a separate calendar entry.

Requests for more than ten consecutive working days require a second
approval from the department head. Requests submitted fewer than fourteen
days before the start date may be declined for staffing reasons.

## Blackout periods

The following periods are closed for vacation because of regulatory
reporting deadlines:

- 1-10 January (year-end close)
- 1-7 April (Q1 regulatory filing)
- 20-31 December (year-end freeze)

Exceptions require written approval from the CFO.

## Sick leave

Sick leave is tracked separately from vacation and is not deducted from the
vacation balance. Report sickness to your manager before 10:00 on the first
day of absence and submit form HRP-207 with a medical certificate if the
absence exceeds three consecutive calendar days.

## Unpaid leave and sabbatical

After three years of continuous service an employee may apply for an unpaid
sabbatical of up to three months using form HRP-219. Approval is at the
discretion of the department head and is subject to headcount planning.

## Payout on termination

Accrued but unused vacation is paid out with the final salary payment.
Negative balances (vacation taken in advance of accrual) are deducted from
the final payment.
